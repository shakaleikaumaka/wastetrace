2Project Overview

Project Name
WasteTrace — the Camp Kitchen Food Loop
Short Summary
An open-source, AI-assisted food-loop system for event kitchens that spots surplus food *before* teardown and routes it to other camps and community partners instead of landfill. It already ran at a real Black Rock City camp kitchen in 2026; this grant builds the cold-chain recovery kit and the documentation so any Regional can replicate it.
Full Description
Event kitchens waste food for one boring reason: nobody knows what's left until it's too late. Purchases happen in bulk, headcounts change, and by teardown the surplus is warm, unsorted, and headed to a dumpster.

WasteTrace closes that loop. It follows food from menu to meal: purchases and receipts go in, daily production draws down live inventory, and the system projects what will be left at end of event — early enough to change the next meal, transfer product to another camp, or schedule refrigerated recovery to a community partner.

This is not a concept. At Black Rock City 2026 our camp kitchen ran its inventory through an AI agent in the camp's group chat: 26 bins, 140 items, 85 food rows and 70 tools tracked live, answering the kitchen within about a minute. A real supply run ($642.96 produce receipt) was traced through the system, and our worked surplus case — 120 lb of onions received, 38 lb used Monday, 27 lb allocated Tuesday, 55 lb flagged as surplus days before teardown — is exactly the decision this system makes early instead of never. We captured 16 operating lessons and 4 kitchen rules during the burn, in the open.

The grant funds two halves of one self-reliant loop. The body: a solar-powered cold-chain kit (12V fridge, insulated food-safe totes, scales, temp loggers) so flagged surplus stays safe long enough to reach people, plus sorting signage and a complete open-source replication kit — field guide, kitchen flow cards, documentation at wastetrace.org. The brain: a compact local-AI unit (NVIDIA DGX Spark, ~240W on camp solar) so the agents run fully on-site — no cloud, no connectivity dependency, no recurring cost — at any Regional. A professional high-volume chef designed the food-service operating model: recipes, purchasing, production, recovery logistics.

Timeline: build + document Nov 2026–Apr 2027, deploy at a sanctioned Regional spring–summer 2027, full-scale at Black Rock City 2027, learnings published as we go.

4Ecological & Systems Impact

Impact
Food is the heaviest, wettest, most methane-productive thing in an event waste stream, and almost all of it was edible a few days earlier. WasteTrace is a circular system in the literal sense: it moves the intervention upstream — from "sort the dumpster" to "never cook the surplus." Early surplus flags change purchasing next time (less trucked in), enable camp-to-camp transfers (resource sharing, one of this grant's named priorities), and route what remains through a refrigerated chain to people instead of landfill. The solar cold-chain makes recovery possible off-grid without generator hours, and running the AI locally on the same solar removes the loop's own footprint too — no datacenter tokens, no connectivity infrastructure, hardware reused event after event. Every pound diverted is also a pound that never rides a vehicle to a transfer station.
Measurement
Quantitative: pounds of surplus flagged before teardown; pounds transferred camp-to-camp; pounds recovered to community partners; meals equivalent; dollars of purchasing avoided event-over-event; forecast accuracy (projected vs. actual leftover); waste-stream weight at teardown vs. our 2026 baseline. The system produces these numbers as a side effect of running — the receipts and inventory ledger ARE the measurement.
Qualitative: number of camps adopting the boards; operating lessons captured in the open (16 lessons + 4 kitchen rules logged live at BRC 2026 — the muscle already exists); participant behavior around sorting and surplus; whether kitchens keep using it at home after the event.

5Community Engagement

Engagement
The interface is a group chat and a public board — the whole camp participates by default. Anyone can ask "what's left?", log a donation, or flag surplus; the kitchen board is readable by every camper. At the event we'll run an open "food loop" walkthrough so other camps can see the flow and take home the printed kit. We start with camps we already know: our neighboring Black Rock City camps (Naked Heart, Cold Yogis, Enthios, and more) are the natural first adoption ring for camp-to-camp transfers at Burning Man 2027. The kitchen loop also isn't a silo: TRACE's sibling agent TERRI (theshellpit.com) ran the rest of camp life at BRC 2026 — crew, shifts, supplies, meeting notes, consent culture docs — in the same group chat, so food coordination plugs into how the camp already talks. Between events, everything lives publicly at wastetrace.org — any community kitchen, food bank drive, or local event can run the same loop. Recovered food goes to community partners chosen for what they can safely accept, which builds year-round civic relationships beyond the burn.

6Open Source & Network Value

Open source
Everything is open by construction — and already published, not promised. Three public repos: github.com/shakaleikaumaka/wastetrace (the nonprofit site plus the complete, deployable chat-agent source any camp can fork — CC0 public domain) · github.com/shakaleikaumaka/shellpit (the full camp playbook plus a FORK-THIS-CAMP kit so any camp can raise its own camp-ops agent — CC0). The field guide, kitchen flow cards, data schemas, board templates, and the full BRC 2026 lessons log publish under open licenses, written so a camp with a spreadsheet and a phone can run the loop — no AI required, the agent just makes it faster. Outreach is wide from day one — we are in outreach mode to support orgs and camps at any Regional burn. Our first-ring adopters are the camps we already camp beside at Black Rock City (Naked Heart, Cold Yogis, Enthios, and more), and we have written to Burning Japan (official Asia Regional) offering both of our camp agents — TRACE, the food loop, and TERRI, the camp-ops agent that kept crew, shifts, and supplies legible at BRC 2026 (theshellpit.com) — as a gift to any camp that will have them. No confirmation yet, and the plan doesn't depend on one; the replication kit will ship with a Japanese translation regardless. We'll share a post-event report with the network (and this program) with real numbers: pounds flagged, transferred, recovered, and what failed.

7Innovation & Inspiration

Innovation
Event sustainability tooling usually starts at the dumpster. This starts at the menu. The inspiring part is watching a kitchen *know its own future*: the system saying "you will have 55 lb of onions left on Thursday" early enough that the camp turns it into soup for a neighboring camp instead of trash — that moment changes what people believe a temporary city can do. It's also, to our knowledge, the first camp kitchen run through a conversational AI agent at a burn — and the playbook works without one.

8Principles & Long-Term Vision

Long-term vision
Prove the loop at Regionals in 2027, publish the numbers, and make the kit standard camp infrastructure across the network. Beyond burns, the same open playbook serves community kitchens and local events year-round; our chef collaborator is building toward food-recovery deployments in the wider food-service world, with the burn network as the proving ground it was designed in. Success = other events running this without us.
2–3 Principles
Leaving No Trace — the project's namesake: waste prevented upstream, not managed downstream. Communal Effort — the loop only works as camps cooking, transferring, and recovering together; the tool is a group chat, not an app silo. Gifting — recovered surplus becomes gifted meals, camp-to-camp and to community partners beyond the trash fence.
