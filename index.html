The Eleventh-Hour Principle

The dumpster is too late

Leaving No Trace is the principle burners live hardest — we crawl the playa for sequins and zip-tie clippings. But the heaviest trace a camp leaves was never MOOP. It's food: bought in bulk, cooked for headcounts that changed, discovered warm and unsorted at teardown, and driven to a landfill where it becomes methane.

Almost everything a kitchen throws away was edible a few days earlier. Leave No Trace doesn't start at the trash fence — it starts at the menu.

WASTE TRACE moves the principle upstream. It follows food from menu to meal, projects what will be left days before teardown, and turns that knowledge into a different next meal, a transfer to a neighboring camp, or a cold-chain recovery run to people who can use it. Waste prevented, not managed.

How the loop works

Menu → meal → no trace

A camp with a spreadsheet and a phone can run it. The AI agent just makes it faster.

Buy

Purchases and receipts go in — the shopping run becomes data the moment it hits the chat.

Cook

Daily production draws down live inventory. The kitchen always knows what's on hand.

Forecast

The system projects what will be left at end of event — while there's still time to act.

Flag

Surplus is flagged days early: change the next meal, or mark it for transfer.

Gift

Flagged food moves — camp-to-camp on playa, or by cold chain to community partners after.
